## 1. Competitive Frame

- **Buyer's current alternative:** Cordell Connect Lite — AUD 577.50/mo, state-wide keyword alerts, 47 matches per email, 3 of which are real re-roofs in the buyer's service area; gated behind a sales call and a quote-only annual contract.
- **Why that alternative falls short:** Cordell sells horizontal coverage to every trade in every state, which structurally prevents it from tuning relevance to a single trade's vocabulary — so a Sydney roofer gets 47 alerts and manually triages 6 hours to find 3 useful ones.
- **Our category claim:** The Sunday-night roofing DA digest for Sydney subbies — the only project-intelligence product built for one trade, one city, delivered once a week at the moment roofers actually quote.

## 2. Value Hierarchy

### Layer 1 — Outcome (what the buyer gets)

Candidates:
1. Stop spending Sunday night checking four council portals. Get the right re-roofs, straight to your phone.
2. Your crew's schedule for next week, without the 6-hour trawl.
3. 5–15 real re-roof leads in your suburbs, every Sunday at 6 pm.

**So-what test:** #1 names the pain moment (Sunday night, four portals); #3 names the deliverable with specificity; #2 is the downstream outcome. **Winner: #3** — most concrete, most repeatable, survives the grandparent test ("what is it?" — "5 to 15 re-roof leads, every Sunday").

### Layer 2 — Mechanism (how we deliver it)

Candidates:
1. We scan all 15 Greater Sydney LGA portals nightly and rank only the jobs a roofer would quote.
2. Roofing-trained relevance layer — not keyword soup — so the noise stays out.
3. We read 500–1,500 DAs a week so you don't have to; only the re-roofs reach you.

**Anyone-could-say-this test:** #2 passes — "roofing-trained" and "not keyword soup" are distinctive and name the alternative's failure mode. #1 is specific but long. **Winner: #2**.

### Layer 3 — Proof (why they should believe us)

Candidates:
1. 70% precision on roofing DAs, tested before we went live — vs Cordell's ~6%.
2. Built by someone who's opened the Penrith DA tracker on a Sunday night. We know the trawl.
3. AUD 199/mo. No sales call. Cancel from your account page. That's the whole deal.

**Distinctiveness test:** #3 names three specific anti-Cordell proofs in one sentence. #2 is the "built by a Sydney subbie" credibility angle — strong for trust, weaker as a repeatable claim. **Winner: #3** for the copy bank; #2 is the "trust signal" subsection.

## 8. Voice & Tone Lock

### Axis choices

| Axis | Choice | Position |
|---|---|---|
| Confident ↔ Humble | **Confident** | Confident-leaning. We make specific, measurable claims. No weasel words, no "we try to" — but we name our own kill switches honestly. Not arrogant. |
| Technical ↔ Plain | **Plain** | Plain-leaning. "Re-roof leads" not "AI-curated relevance-ranked project-intelligence signals." Tech is in the product, not in the copy. |
| Warm ↔ Formal | **Warm** | Warm-leaning. "Subbies" not "subcontractors." "No drama" not "frictionless." Sunday-evening register, not Monday-morning corporate. |
| Witty ↔ Sober | **Sober** | Sober, but specific. "47 alerts, 3 useful" is more damning than a joke. Wit comes from precision, not from puns. |
| Specific ↔ Abstract | **Specific** | Aggressively specific. Every claim names a number, a time, an LGA, a price. "Curated" without a number is banned. |

### Voice model

**Closest brand reference: Linear** — direct, sober, specific, no hype. Linear writes "fast" without adjectives because the product is fast. We write "15 LGAs, 5–15 leads, Sunday 6 pm" without adjectives because the specificity is the copy.

**Second reference for warmth register: Plain (plain.com)** — customer-support tool whose voice is "clear, human, no-fuss." They write to practitioners, not to C-suites.

The synthesised voice: **a roofie talking to a roofie.** Not a SaaS vendor talking to a buyer. Eli knows what a DA is; we don't explain it. Eli knows what a re-roof scope looks like; we name "Colorbond replacement" not "residential roofing project." The voice implies peer credibility without claiming it overtly.

### Vocabulary

**Use:**
- subbies, roofers, DA, re-roof, LGA, portal, digest, Sunday night
- "no drama", "no sales call", "cancel anytime"
- specific numbers: "15 LGAs", "5–15 leads", "AUD 199/mo", "60 seconds"

**Avoid:**
- platform, solution, leverage, unlock, seamless, empower, next-generation
- "AI-powered" (tech is the mechanism, not the value prop — say "trained on roofing language" instead)
- "world-class", "industry-leading", "comprehensive"
- exclamation marks in product UI (fine in marketing email subject lines sparingly)
- passive voice: "leads are delivered" → "we deliver 5–15 leads"

### Test 2 — The-screenshot test

> *Could Cordell, LeadManager, or EstimateOne screenshot the hero and put it on their own page with a logo swap?*

**Result: Pass (fail-proof).** Cordell cannot claim "15 Sydney LGAs" (they sell state-wide), "Sunday 6 pm" (no digest cadence), "AUD 199/mo" (Lite starts at AUD 577.50/mo), or "roofing only" (horizontal product). EstimateOne cannot claim "DA digest" (tender-only). No competitor can lift this copy intact. The wedge axis (Niche) is load-bearing in every headline.

### Test 3 — The-redaction test

> *Redact "ProjectIntelligence AU" from every section. Can a careful reader still tell what category we're in and who we're for?*

**Result: Pass.** "Sunday roofing digest", "Sydney subbies", "re-roof DAs", "15 LGAs", "AUD 199/mo" — the category (weekly DA digest for roofing subbies) and ICP (Sydney roofing owner-operator) are recoverable from the copy alone. The product name is not load-bearing.

### Test 4 — The-anti-axis test

> *Does the copy ever claim a benefit on the anti-axis (coverage breadth, multi-trade, contact-data depth, head-contractor flow, free-tier race-to-zero)?*

**Result: Pass.** The anti-positioning section (§7) explicitly names what we don't claim.
